## What you are looking at

This specimen comes from a survey point cloud used in my existing geospatial portfolio. The animation renders 43,523 measured points. It is a view of real geometry, rather than a generated example or a product mock-up.

The display copy is centred on a local origin. Original coordinate offsets, inspection photographs, observation notes, and identifiers are not included. Animation colours encode relative elevation within the sample; they do not represent defects, material condition, or an engineering classification.

The interactive STL contains a deterministic sample of 12,000 of those points. Each is represented by a small tetrahedral marker so it can be displayed in GitHub's native 3D viewer. These markers are display glyphs: they are not a reconstructed asset surface. The STL uses a uniform 5× display scale to make the specimen visible in the default viewer. It has no point colours, and it is intended for visual exploration rather than measurement or engineering use.

## Why this work matters

A point cloud is a starting point. The useful work is registering and checking the data, understanding what can be measured, relating observations to the asset, and producing something another person can use.

That is also how I approach software: make complex information easier to inspect, keep evidence connected to findings, and reduce the manual work between capture and delivery.
